How LLM-GC works

Every request your app makes carries the whole conversation again, so the prompt grows with every message. LLM-GC is a garbage collector for that conversation: it keeps the prompt from growing without limit, and shows exactly what the model can still see. Numbers in this colour are your proxy's settings.

The three tabs

  • Conversation: what the model remembers, what it has forgotten, and whether to worry; then its facts, a map, and every exchange, newest first unless you switch it, with what the model reads for it.
  • Requests: the Claude Code session turn by turn, each request it made, and what it cost by the provider's own count.
  • Engine: when the next collection comes and why, why each exchange is at its level, and every check.

Hover any ⓘ or dotted phrase for a short meaning; click an ⓘ for the full one, at the foot of this guide.

What one request carries

  1. your client's own; LLM-GC never changes them
  2. the old generation: every older exchange at its level, once LLM-GC applies a collection
  3. the young generation: at least the newest … messages, as written
  4. as your client sent it

Click a part for its full meaning. Until LLM-GC applies its first collection, it sends each request exactly as your client wrote it. After that, your client's instructions from older messages open the recap, word for word.

The provider caches a request from its first byte. LLM-GC never changes the system prompt or tools, so they stay cached. A collection changes the recap, so the provider caches everything from the recap onwards again.

When a collection happens

A collection (a cut in the code) has two steps, because each step costs something different.

  1. Prepare. After every reply, LLM-GC runs a check (a pass in the code). It prepares a collection when the older messages to summarise or archive reach … tokens, and the result would be smaller. Preparing costs LLM-GC's own model calls, to write summaries and read facts. It does not change what the model is sent. The newest … messages always stay recent.
  2. Apply. LLM-GC applies the prepared collection to a later request, when one of these is true:
    • The provider's cache is cold. The provider reads the whole request anyway, so the change costs nothing extra.
    • The cache is warm, and the collection saves at least … the tokens the provider must cache again.
    • The stale tokens reach … of the window. These are older messages the model still gets word for word. LLM-GC then applies the collection, even on a warm cache.
  3. LLM-GC never applies a collection that makes the prompt bigger. While the model is using tools, it applies none until the model finishes. Run GC now prepares a collection and applies it at once. It skips the token minimum and the cache, but not these two rules.
Warm and cold, in plain words. The provider is like a reviewer who has just read the first 50 pages of your document. Send it back soon with one new page and they read only the new page: the cache is warm. Send it after they have forgotten and they read all 51: it is cold. Change page 1 and they must reread everything, remembered or not. A collection changes page 1, so LLM-GC applies one when the reviewer has forgotten anyway, or when the saving is worth the reread.

What to watch

  1. Out of view: the recap reached its budget with every exchange already at its least detail, so the oldest were folded behind one line. The model knows they exist, not what was said. An ordinary conversation never gets there.
  2. A dot on a message: it changes form at the next collection, in the colour it will become. Earlier exchanges move too: up when the conversation comes back to them, down when it has moved on.
  3. Search says whether the model still receives the words you type, and if not, what it reads instead.
  4. Should you worry?, at the top of the Conversation tab: everything worth a look, such as messages out of view, facts the model can't see, or part of a collection that fell short. Each links to its evidence; nothing is lost. Dismiss one with × and it stays hidden until there is more of it.
  5. The two chips beside the picker: the provider's cache, warm with the time left or cold, and the next collection, from "not enough yet" to "applied with next request". Click the second for the Next collection card.
  6. Two counts of tokens: LLM-GC's own counts are estimates by its own tokenizer; the provider's count of the last request is what it bills. The page names whose count each figure is.
